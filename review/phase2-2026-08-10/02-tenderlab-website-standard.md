# TenderLab Website Standard

## Brand and positioning

TenderLab is a specialist UK health and social care tender-writing and bid consultancy. Its defining behaviour is qualification first: establish whether the published requirements, delivery model, evidence and commercial position appear supportable before recommending a full writing engagement.

The website must communicate a precise, candid, calm, forensic and humane practice. It must never resemble a generic marketing agency, an indiscriminate “win any tender” service or an automated content factory.

## Visual system

- Warm editorial base: cream, ink navy, evidence red/coral, pale blue, sage and restrained yellow.
- Editorial collage imagery tailored to the service or care setting, using paper, documentary evidence and a red-thread motif where meaningful.
- Compact, information-rich composition with genuine breathing room; no aggressively large cards, giant empty heroes or one-word mobile text columns.
- A stable grid and spacing scale, but varied section rhythm: editorial split, evidence strip, comparison matrix, annotated process, testimonial, article grid and conversion panel.
- Rounded geometry is used selectively to soften the sector, not as the default container for every object.
- No dark-mode sections, arbitrary glows, meaningless diagrams, fake dashboards or generic AI imagery.

## Typography

- Sans serif is the primary interface and reading face; serif is reserved only where it contributes editorial character without impairing comprehension.
- Body copy remains between approximately 45 and 75 characters per line.
- Headings use moderate responsive scales and balanced line breaks; mobile headings must not create stacked single-word columns.
- Small labels remain readable and are never used to hide material information.
- Sentence fragments are permitted only for recognised labels, not as a default writing style.

## Information architecture and UX

- Primary navigation: Services, Care settings, Live tenders, Case studies, Blogs, About, Contact.
- Menus close after selection and route changes begin at the top of the new page.
- Cards are clickable as complete units when they represent a destination.
- Every page answers: where am I, what is this, is it relevant, why trust it, what can I do next?
- Primary actions use plain language and a minimum 44 px target.
- Mobile is intentionally composed, not a shrunken desktop.

## Content and claims

- British English, complete sentences, clear subjects and concrete mechanisms.
- Copy speaks to owners, directors, registered managers, nominated individuals and bid leads without teaching an imaginary third party.
- The site explains suitability, limitations, process, comparisons, pricing approach and risk openly.
- “92%”, “200+”, “£50M+” and “5/5” are four separate historic measures and must be qualified near use.
- No guarantee, “maximum marks”, universal deadline, invented score, unverified turnaround or unsupported superlative.
- TenderLab improves how real evidence is selected and communicated; it does not invent operational evidence.

## Conversion and trust

- The primary conversion is a relevant enquiry; secondary conversions are reviewing live tenders, comparing services, reading proof and calling/emailing.
- Qualification-first honesty is a trust feature and must be stated without implying a guaranteed outcome.
- Verified reviews and case evidence sit near the claims they support.
- Contact forms are light, visible, accessible and prefilled when entered from a specific tender.
- No manipulative urgency or hidden information.

## Search, accessibility and engineering

- One useful primary intent per indexable page; consolidate trivial variants.
- Every indexable page: HTTP 200, crawlable, canonical, internally linked, rendered and included in the appropriate sitemap.
- Use descriptive metadata and anchors, accurate structured data and complete redirect protection.
- WCAG 2.2 AA is the baseline, including keyboard access, focus, labels, contrast, reflow and reduced motion.
- Use server components by default; client JavaScript only where interaction requires it.
- Optimise imagery, fonts and third-party scripts; prevent layout shift.
- Preview deployments remain noindex and disconnected from production until the launch gate passes.
