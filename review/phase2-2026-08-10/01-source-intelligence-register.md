# TenderLab Source Intelligence Register

This register converts the usable professional library and current authorities into implementation rules. It is a control document, not a substitute for the website.

| Discipline | Source and location | Applicable principle | TenderLab rule | Failure condition | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand | Wheeler, *Designing Brand Identity*, brand ideals and process | Meaning, differentiation and coherence must survive every touchpoint. | TenderLab must consistently present as a qualification-first health and social care tender specialist, not a generic copywriting agency. | A page could be rebranded for any consultancy without changing its meaning. | Page-by-page proposition and component review. |
| Brand | Neumeier, *The Brand Gap*, differentiation and validation | A brand is the customer’s perception; distinctiveness must be experienced, not merely claimed. | Use candid qualification, evaluator-visible evidence and care-sector fluency as recurring behaviour and proof. | Superlatives or visual novelty replace credible difference. | Copy, journey and proof audit. |
| Brand | Keller, *Strategic Brand Management*, positioning and customer-based brand equity | Salience, performance, imagery, judgements and trust build durable equity. | Make category, audience, service competence and evidence recognisable within the first screen and reinforce them near decisions. | Visitors cannot explain what TenderLab does, for whom or why it is credible. | Five-second comprehension test and content inventory. |
| Graphic design | Ambrose & Harris, *The Fundamentals of Graphic Design*, hierarchy, grid and composition | Hierarchy and alignment should make relationships immediately legible. | Use a stable editorial grid, restrained scale changes and compact modules with deliberate focal points. | Oversized type, empty cards or inconsistent alignments obscure the information. | Desktop, tablet and mobile visual QA. |
| Graphic design | Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, hierarchy and readability | Type is an information system; line length, leading and scale must support reading. | Keep body copy at readable widths, moderate weights and responsive headings that do not create one-word columns. | Text collides, becomes excessively bold or breaks into narrow stacks. | Responsive screenshots at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px. |
| Graphic design | Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (image-only supplied scan, visually inspected) | Consistent grid relationships create order and rhythm. | Use repeatable columns and spacing tokens while varying section composition to avoid monotony. | Elements appear arbitrarily placed or every section repeats the same card grid. | Overlay/grid inspection across templates. |
| UX | Krug, *Don’t Make Me Think*, navigation, scanning and usability testing | Interfaces should be self-evident, scannable and conventionally navigable. | Use plain labels: Services, Care settings, Live tenders, Case studies, Blogs, About and Contact. Make the entire relevant card clickable. | Users must infer labels, hunt for arrows or wonder whether navigation worked. | Keyboard, pointer and route tests. |
| UX | Web Style Guide, information architecture, page structure and interface design | Structure follows user tasks and content relationships. | Organise pages around choosing support, assessing fit, finding an opportunity, reviewing proof and contacting TenderLab. | Navigation mirrors internal company jargon rather than user intent. | Journey testing and crawl map. |
| UX | Laws of UX: Jakob’s Law, Hick’s Law, Fitts’s Law, Von Restorff Effect | Familiar controls, limited choices, adequate targets and one clear emphasis reduce decision cost. | Keep main navigation concise, make primary actions visually distinct and maintain minimum 44 px interactive targets. | Competing CTAs, tiny arrows or overloaded menus impair decisions. | Interaction and target-size audit. |
| Content | Sheridan, *They Ask, You Answer* | Address price, problems, comparisons and buyer questions openly. | Provide candid decision support on suitability, service choice, process, risk and pricing approach without hiding behind a sales call. | Pages avoid the questions buyers need answered before enquiry. | Intent-to-page coverage review. |
| Content | Heath & Heath, *Made to Stick* | Useful messages are simple, concrete and credible. | Lead with one memorable proposition and support it with specific mechanisms and qualified evidence. | Abstract claims, buzzwords or unexplained statistics dominate. | Editorial and truth-register review. |
| Content | Handley, *Everybody Writes* | Useful, human and carefully edited writing earns trust. | Use British English, complete sentences, active voice and sector-specific detail; remove robotic fragments and filler. | Repetitive two-word fragments, generic AI cadence or keyword stuffing appears. | Content lint and manual editorial review. |
| Content design | GOV.UK interface writing; ONS user needs, scanning, structure and plain language | Start from user need, front-load meaning and make actions unambiguous. | Put the user’s decision and required next step before internal process detail. | Introductory sections delay the page’s real content or use vague CTA labels. | Task-based content review. |
| PR | Theaker, *The Public Relations Handbook*, stakeholder and reputation management | Reputation is built through consistent, defensible relationships and communication. | Claims must remain defensible to clients, commissioners, competitors, partners and journalists. | A claim cannot be supported or is stated more strongly than the evidence. | Business Truth Register and claim checks. |
| PR | Cutlip, Center & Broom, *Effective Public Relations*, two-way communication | Credible organisations listen, respond and make feedback visible. | Use verified client reviews, transparent contact routes, clear process and useful enquiry feedback. | Testimonials are invented, anonymised without reason or disconnected from claims. | Source-link and form-delivery checks. |
| PR | Seitel, *The Practice of Public Relations*, ethical communication | Accuracy and legitimacy take priority over promotional convenience. | Do not imply guarantees, partnerships or direct work that cannot be evidenced. | “Guaranteed”, “maximum marks”, or unqualified relationship claims appear. | Repository-wide prohibited-claim scan. |
| SEO | *The Art of SEO*, Fourth Edition, intent, architecture, crawling, internal links and measurement | Search performance begins with intent-aligned architecture and technically accessible, useful pages. | Map one primary intent to each authoritative page; consolidate trivial variants and link clusters contextually. | Thin duplicate routes, doorway pages, orphan pages or conflicting canonicals exist. | Crawl, sitemap and intent-map audit. |
| Search | Google Search Essentials and helpful-content guidance | Build people-first, crawlable content and avoid scaled low-value or manipulative pages. | Every indexable page needs a distinct useful purpose, accurate metadata and visible original content. | Pages exist primarily to capture keyword variants or make misleading claims. | Rendered crawl and page-purpose review. |
| Search | Google SEO Starter Guide and how Search works | Descriptive titles, links, crawlability and internal discovery support understanding. | Use descriptive titles, headings, anchors, canonicals, sitemap entries and 200 responses for indexable pages. | Missing titles, blocked resources, accidental noindex or orphan routes. | Built-site contract tests and HTTP crawl. |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 AA, current W3C quick reference | Perceivable, operable, understandable and robust interaction is a baseline. | Semantic landmarks, logical headings, visible focus, sufficient contrast, labels, errors, reflow and reduced motion are mandatory. | Keyboard traps, low contrast, missing labels, hidden focus or horizontal overflow. | Automated checks plus manual keyboard/reflow testing. |
| Accessibility | GOV.UK accessibility and inclusive-design guidance | Accessibility must be designed and manually tested, not added at the end. | Shared components must pass before page templates are approved. | A global component creates repeated barriers. | Header, menu, form, accordion and footer component audits. |
| Performance | Google Core Web Vitals documentation and Web Style Guide implementation principles | Responsiveness and stability affect experience and search quality. | Optimise hero images, fonts and scripts; avoid unnecessary client JavaScript and layout shifts. | Oversized source assets, blocking scripts, unstable media or excessive animation. | Lighthouse and deployed runtime measurement. |
