# TenderLab market, search and launch strategy

Date: 3 September 2026  
Market: United Kingdom  
Scope: tender writing and bid consultancy, health and social care specialisms, live-tender discovery and pipeline products

## The decision

TenderLab should not compete as another general bid-writing agency and should not attempt to win 304 search intents by publishing 304 near-duplicate pages. It should own the intersection of three capabilities:

1. specialist UK health and social care procurement knowledge;
2. evaluator-readable bid writing supported by documented evidence;
3. a live opportunity and pipeline layer that converts tender discovery into bid/no-bid decisions and writing support.

That position is more defensible than a generic “high win rate” promise. It also creates a connected acquisition journey:

`care-setting question → specialist guidance → relevant live tenders → viability check → bid writing/review → documented result`

The accompanying `KEYWORD-OPPORTUNITY-MAP.csv` contains 304 distinct search queries mapped to a smaller set of strong destination pages.

## Competitive takeaways

### What is now table stakes

- a care-sector or public-sector specialism claim;
- live tender discovery or alerts;
- a published process;
- case studies, reviews and win-rate claims;
- free consultation or eligibility check;
- sector pages for domiciliary care, supported living, children’s services, mental health, complex care and residential/nursing care.

### Where TenderLab can create separation

- **Evidence transparency:** show redacted award evidence, TenderLab’s exact role, the procurement, result, denominator and limitations.
- **Evaluator usability:** explain how an answer is mapped to the question, scoring descriptors, specification and available proof.
- **Care-delivery translation:** connect regulation, staffing, safeguarding, governance, mobilisation and outcomes to specific buyer requirements.
- **Opportunity-to-action continuity:** keep the correct care-setting filter from the landing page through live tenders, saving, consultation and service selection.
- **Decision usefulness:** help a provider decide whether to bid, not merely persuade it to buy writing.
- **Fresh procurement intelligence:** add commentary derived from current notices, award notices and commissioning plans, with primary-source citations.

## Page strategy

### Pages to strengthen before launch

1. `/services/bid-writing`: scope, inputs, controls, outputs, review stages, turnaround constraints and examples.
2. `/services/pre-submission-review`: scoring method, annotated output, prioritisation and who it is for.
3. `/services/tender-readiness-audit` and `/services/bid-viability`: explicit decision criteria and worked examples.
4. `/services/tender-pipeline-monitoring`: sources monitored, matching rules, exclusions, alert frequency and human triage.
5. `/tenders`: source coverage, data freshness, saved-tender behaviour and routes to filtered care settings.
6. Six tender hubs: current opportunity data plus unique buyer, eligibility and evidence guidance.
7. `/case-studies` and `/proof`: named procurement context, TenderLab’s role, evidence boundaries and comparable methodology.
8. `/about`: named accountable people and verifiable professional background when the business is ready to publish them.

### Evidence-led guides to prioritise

- how TenderLab makes a bid/no-bid decision;
- how to read a care tender scoring grid;
- what commissioners can verify in a care method statement;
- framework, dynamic market and call-off strategy;
- CQC and Ofsted eligibility gates with correct scope distinctions;
- Procurement Act 2023 implications for care providers;
- tender-writing cost and scope, with transparent inclusions/exclusions;
- lost-bid diagnosis using evaluator feedback;
- current care-commissioning pattern reports based on real notice data;
- public-sector portal and submission checklist.

Each guide must contain original analysis, a named review owner when available, primary-source references, publication/review dates and useful internal links. Google explicitly says unique, non-commodity, people-first content matters more than attempts to manufacture every query variation.

## GEO and AI visibility strategy

1. Keep all important explanations in server-rendered HTML.
2. Keep OAI-SearchBot and other discovery crawlers crawlable; measure `utm_source=chatgpt.com` referral traffic.
3. Maintain Organization, WebSite, Service, Article, CollectionPage, Breadcrumb and visible FAQ markup only where it matches the page.
4. Give every claim a definition, source boundary and date.
5. Add concise, directly answerable sections to substantive pages, but do not fragment prose purely for AI systems.
6. Publish primary-source-backed procurement analysis and expert interpretation that an answer engine can cite.
7. Earn real references from care associations, client organisations, procurement publications and professional profiles; do not buy or simulate mentions.
8. Measure Google AI visibility through Search Console’s generative AI reporting and AI referrals in analytics.

`llms.txt` is retained for services that choose to use it. Google’s current official guidance says it neither helps nor harms Google Search because Google ignores it.

## 90-day execution plan

### Before production release

- connect Stripe, Calendly, Google Calendar/Meet and transactional email under the TenderLab account;
- complete one free booking and one Stripe test payment end to end;
- verify webhook receipt, booking notification, calendar event and Meet link;
- complete the final crawl, structured-data, accessibility and mobile checks;
- establish Search Console and analytics baselines before changing URLs or content.

### Days 1–30

- strengthen the four highest-commercial-intent service pages;
- publish two high-evidence decision guides;
- add named accountability/reviewer information where factually approved;
- introduce query-to-consultation conversion tracking;
- start weekly indexing, query and AI-referral reporting.

### Days 31–60

- improve all six live-tender hubs with distinct procurement guidance;
- publish the first quarterly care-procurement data report;
- expand documented case studies with redacted primary evidence;
- begin legitimate association, partner and client-reference outreach.

### Days 61–90

- add saved tenders and subscriber alerts only after product/privacy requirements are approved;
- create comparison content based on verifiable workflows and user needs, not unsupported attacks;
- refresh underperforming pages from Search Console query evidence;
- decide whether any regional page merits publication based on original tender and award data.

## Measurement and release gates

### Commercial

- consultation starts and completed bookings by landing page;
- qualified enquiries by care setting and service;
- live-tender-to-consultation conversion;
- paid checkout creation, completion and abandonment;
- lead-to-instruction rate and instructed value.

### Search and GEO

- valid indexed pages versus sitemap pages;
- impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position by query cluster;
- number of target queries in top 3, top 10 and top 20;
- Google AI feature visibility and cited pages;
- ChatGPT referral sessions and conversions;
- branded search growth and verified third-party citations.

### Guardrails

- no promise of first position or guaranteed awards;
- no invented authors, credentials, reviews, awards or outcome evidence;
- no programmatic doorway pages for keyword variants or regions;
- no production launch while payment, scheduling and notifications are unverified;
- no indexable preview or staging URLs.
